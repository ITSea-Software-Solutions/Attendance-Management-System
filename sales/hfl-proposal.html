Points worth your attention

Observation (from your own file)Why it matters
One overtime rate is inconsistent. 60 of 61 workers with OT are paid at Stipend÷26÷8. One worker (Vilas Chaudhari) is calculated at ÷12 — ₹8,077 instead of ₹12,115. A ₹4,038 difference in a single cycle from one cell. If it has been repeating, it is roughly ₹48,000 a year. Either a typing slip or a special arrangement — but nothing in the sheet tells you which.
Overtime recorded against absent days. 10 entries carry 41.5 OT hours on days the same worker is marked "A". Payment for hours on a day the person was not present. The system rejects this combination at entry.
889.5 OT hours (20% of all OT) fall on weekly-offs, across 90 entries. Legitimate, but there is no record of who authorised working on a day off. That is ~₹62,000 of payment resting on verbal approval.
Attendance is self-declared. P/A/WO is typed by a supervisor; 314 absent marks carry no evidence. No proof of who was actually at the gate. This is the single biggest exposure in third-party labour — and the hardest to defend in an audit or a contractor dispute.
The two files do not connect. None of the 65 names in the muster appear in the contractor master. The muster carries no Aadhaar, no contractor and no joining date, so you cannot split cost per contractor or prove identity from it.
Full 12-digit Aadhaar numbers sit in an open spreadsheet, and one mobile number has already been corrupted by Excel (7.06922E+11). Employee codes are the last 4 digits of Aadhaar. Under the DPDP Act 2023 this is identity data in an uncontrolled file. TrueCrew stores a masked Aadhaar only, with the full number never saved and biometrics encrypted.
The commercial point. This cycle alone is ₹11.81 lakh, of which ₹3.12 lakh is overtime. On an annual basis that is roughly ₹1.4 crore of contract-labour spend controlled by a spreadsheet that anyone can retype. A 1% leakage — one ghost shift, one wrong rate, one unapproved OT block — is about ₹1.4 lakh a year. The system costs a fraction of that.

3. Built for your sheet INCLUDED IN THIS QUOTE

The following is developed to match your existing workings, so your month-end output does not change:

  • 26-to-25 wage cycle as a configurable pay period (not calendar month).
  • The same muster grid you use now — worker × day, with P / A / WO codes and the daily OT row beneath — generated automatically from gate punches and downloadable as Excel.
  • Wage master per worker — stipend band, divisor (26) and OT divisor (8), so rates live in the system instead of being retyped each month.
  • Automatic wage computation — present days, OT hours, OT amount, arrears and net payable, using the exact formula above.
  • Overtime with approval — OT is derived from real IN/OUT times, and any manual OT or work on a weekly-off requires an approver, with a record of who approved it.
  • Weekly-off and holiday calendar, so an off day is never confused with an absence.
  • Contractor-wise bill verification — days, hours, OT and payable split by contractor, to check against the invoice each contractor raises.
  • Exception report — absent-with-OT, missing OUT, rate mismatches and unapproved OT flagged before payroll is released.

Payroll statutory processing (PF/ESI/PT returns) is deliberately out of scope — the system produces the verified attendance and wage figures your existing payroll process consumes.
